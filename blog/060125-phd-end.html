End of my PhD Journey

06.01.2025

A few musings on wrapping up my PhD journey.

Academic PhD Stats Costco

TLDR: I love collecting data about my interactions with the world. Here are a few, important (to me), stats from my PhD journey.

I started my Ph.D. journey nearly five years ago. In hindsight, I can easily say it was an adventure in itself, but also the pathway I hoped it would be to allow me to work on interesting research and develop my skills as a scholar.

This spring marked the culmination of that journey, a milestone reached in what feels like no time at all. In the past year alone, I've been swept into a whirlwind of activities: finalizing the analysis for my dissertation's final study, networking and presenting at multiple conferences and doctoral consortia, navigating the academic job market, and completing the dissertation write-up itself. I also made it a point to enjoy my time in the DMV by exploring new restaurants, farmers markets, and importantly, coffee shops to keep me going through the writing.

I love collecting data, and what better way to summarize the past quinquennium than present a few interesting stats I've collected over this period. While my journey is unique, these insights might offer you (potential student, faculty member, or just curious reader) something to think about. This is my narrative, not advice on the “correct” way (lol) to do a PhD, nor would I use this as a metric to compare to your own situation (your Costco hotdog and pizza intake should be a deeply personal decision). Think of it as a story from someone that loves applying to things, even when he feels underqualified.

The data I present are drawn from various tracking tools that I've relied upon regularly over the years. I mention the source for each item below.

A record of applications, submissions, and more

During my time at community college, I heard the phrase (though I can't remember who said it): "If you're not getting many rejections, you're staying in the bounds of what people think you can do." As someone who, until that point, was deeply afraid of being rejected for the implications of not being good enough, I was shook by hearing this point of view.

It is arguable to what extent it is true, but what I can say is that the philosophy has altered how I view my own capabilities and has encouraged me to apply it to everything. In fact, I don't think I would have made it this far without it.

Figure 1 presents a breakdown of all collective applications and submissions I have made throughout my PhD. I am proud of everything I have achieved, but I think it is important to acknowledge that there are many folders filled with applications, proposals, and more that didn't get funded. Such is part of the game.
